Privacy

Everything you ride and record stays on your Mac. There is no account and no analytics. While Anifora is in beta it sends reports about how it ran — never your rides — and you can switch that off.

A few things do use the internet. They are listed below, with what is sent and who gets it.

What stays on your Mac

  • Your rides: power, cadence, heart rate, speed and the second-by-second recording, as files.
  • Your rider profile: name, weight, height, FTP and heart-rate figures.
  • Your roads and the GPX files you import, your workouts, and your stars and times for roads.
  • What your trainer, power meter and heart-rate strap say over Bluetooth. It is read by Anifora and goes nowhere.

The folders are ~/Documents/TrainerApp and ~/Library/Application Support/TrainerApp. If iCloud Drive keeps your Documents folder in sync, your Mac copies that folder to iCloud. That is your Mac’s setting, not Anifora’s.

What leaves your Mac, and who gets it

1. Looking for updates, and downloading them: GitHub

Once a day, unless you switch it off in Settings (⌘,), and never during a ride. The list of versions is a page on GitHub Pages (sotirios-efstathiou.github.io). A new version is downloaded from GitHub (github.com).

GitHub sees your IP address, the time, and the name and version of the app in the request, as with any web request. Anifora sends no system profile: not your Mac’s model, macOS version or memory. It sends no ride and nothing about you. GitHub’s own privacy statement covers what it does with its logs.

2. Maps: Apple

The 3D world and the map picture are Apple Maps, through MapKit. Apple sees the area being shown, as it does for any map in any app. Anifora does not send Apple your rides. See Apple’s privacy policy.

3. The street and finer heights for a road: OpenStreetMap and national laser surveys, only if you say yes

Anifora can line a road you import up with the streets of OpenStreetMap, so it is drawn on the street it follows; for a road in North Rhine-Westphalia, France, Switzerland or Austria, a laser survey can also give finer heights. Anifora asks the first time it could help, and never looks anything up at launch. The answer is kept; you can change it in About Anifora, and a road's … menu lines up one you imported before.

If you say yes, Anifora sends the 4 km map squares the road passes through, the same squares to two services: to the survey for that country for the heights — Geobasis NRW (wcs.nrw.de), IGN (data.geopf.fr), swisstopo (data.geo.admin.ch) or BEV (data.bev.gv.at) — and to the OpenStreetMap Overpass API (overpass-api.de) for the streets and their bridges, tunnels and galleries. The road is fitted to the streets on your Mac. Where a survey comes in tiles of its own, Anifora fetches every tile under the square, not only those the road crosses. It never sends the track itself and never a ride. The request names the app and its version (Anifora/…) and keeps no cookies. The answers are kept on your Mac, in ~/Library/Caches/TrainerApp, so a road is asked about once. The services see your IP address, as with any web request.

4. Problem reports: your own email, only when you send one

Help → Report a Problem… prepares a file with Anifora’s version, your Mac’s model and macOS version, Anifora’s own log and crash reports with your user name taken out. It never includes your rides or heart rate. It opens an email to Sotirios.Efstathiou@gmail.com with the file attached. Anifora sends nothing itself: the email leaves when you press Send, through your own mail account.

5. Beta reports: the developer’s own server, unless you switch them off

While Anifora is in beta it sends reports about how it ran, so problems that happen only on your Mac can be found and fixed. The welcome screen says so (or, if you already had Anifora, a note on its front page), and nothing is sent before you have seen that. They are on unless you switch them off in Settings (⌘,) → Beta reports.

A report says: Anifora’s version; your Mac’s model, macOS version and memory; a random number made on your Mac that names no one but tells your reports apart from another tester’s; how long Anifora ran and whether it quit normally; how much memory it used and whether it stopped responding; what went wrong (a trainer not responding, a file it could not read, a crash); Anifora’s own log of what it did, which names the roads and workouts you loaded and the Bluetooth devices it saw; and the crash and hang reports macOS writes about Anifora. Your user name, your Mac’s name and your home folder are taken out first.

It never contains a ride, a heart rate, a position, your name, weight or FTP. It is never sent during a ride. Each report waits as a plain file you can open — Settings → Show Them — and a file you delete there is not sent.

Reports go to a small server of the developer’s own, run on Cloudflare (workers.dev), and are read only by the developer. The server keeps the report and nothing else: not your IP address. Cloudflare sees your IP address, as with any web request. The server deletes every report 180 days after it arrived.

That is all. Apart from these five, Anifora makes no connections of its own. It has no tracking, no advertising and no account to sign in to.

Your choices

  • Switch off the daily update check in Settings. Then Anifora never contacts GitHub unless you choose Check for Updates….
  • Answer no to survey heights, or change your answer in About Anifora.
  • Do not send a problem report.
  • Switch off beta reports in Settings (⌘,). Nothing more is sent from then on.
  • To remove everything Anifora keeps, quit it and delete the two folders above, and the TrainerApp folder in ~/Library/Caches.
